## My thoughts on AI so far

Despite pushback and counter arguments against the use of AI in professional and everyday spaces, I strongly believe this emerging frontier has a profound and positive effect on modern society.

Anecdotal evidence has shown me how freely-available AI search and generative tools dramatically increase the rate of content discovery for both educational and creative endeavours.
While yes, current iterations safely available to the public are lacking in long-term coherence, and oft-times accuracy, it certainly beats the total time and effort required to research the issue at hand directly through search engines, and even slower, through books.

So far, my gentle addition of AI into basic workflows includes the use of privacy-focused AI solutions: Proton's Lumo, and Duckduckgo's Duck.AI

Privacy remains a real concern for me regarding the use of remotely deployed AI, however for everyday and uncritical research, these privacy-focused AI platforms seem to effectively provide a layer of obfuscation in that searches are carried out by the AI agent, often refining the search terms to better match the inquiry, while remaining more accurate and rapid enough as a process to warrant a permanent stay in reflexive use versus heading to the search boxes alone.

Can AI completely replace people in work? 

In some areas, it's happening - not largely due to the callousness of rapid innovation in business, but more as a result of a desperate need to provide critical services for a functioning society; that is to say an increase in the use of automation - to the effective replacement of people - in high-skill and low-supply roles such as medical diagnostics. 

I do believe people will continue to engage in creative exercises, as evident here in my manually written personal blog posts, and AI has certainly had a tangible effect in the professional environment, as I hear from friends in health services. Making work lighter is a constant objective in scaling societies, and to me, a working AI solution, as a cognitive multiplier, makes for a giddy fantasy of mass-produced core services ( super cheap and super good healthcare ?.. ).

Floating down to normal life stuff, post-primary students have used AI software to design exams and learning material with promising results and progress - the large language models do seem to teach language and essay-writing well enough to a standard that's at least better than no practice at all!

Another relevant frontier is the advancement of robotics, providing a continously evolving algorithm with a body to enact whatever it wills, or whatever it's told to do!

Although the emergence of holistically robust and world-changing AI technology seems scary, I advocate for an AI era that expands the landscape of education and healthcare for the benefit of present and future humans to come.
